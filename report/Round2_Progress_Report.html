1 Dataset Description, Curation & Statistics

Why this dataset? Mainstream NLP stance benchmarks (SemEval-2016, VAST) almost exclusively focus on English. For low-resource South Asian languages like Hindi (Devanagari, ~600M speakers) and Bengali (Eastern Nagari, ~300M speakers), climate stance data is virtually non-existent despite high vulnerability to extreme weather events.

Curated Corpus: We constructed an expert-balanced corpus of 3,200 labeled samples (plus 320 held-out hidden evaluation samples) across 11 diverse environmental subcategories: deforestation, glacier retreat, renewable energy, floods/cyclones, heatwaves, carbon taxes, vehicular pollution, climate denial myths, and conversational banter.

Realistic Noise Engine: Real Indic social media text is notoriously noisy. We injected a calibrated multi-tier noise pipeline:

  • 35% Clean: Standard formal grammar, danda punctuation (।).
  • 50% Light Noise: Matra elongations ("गर्मिइइ"), ASCII punctuation (!!??), sentiment emojis (🌍, 🌱, 🤡, 🙄, 🔥).
  • 15% Heavy Noise: Sarcastic negation, phonemic substitutions, and colloquial mixed constructs.

2 Current Methodology, Models & Technical Workflow

Our methodology spans an end-to-end, reproducible 6-phase engineering pipeline implementing Classical ML, Deep Pretrained Transformers, and Soft-Voting Ensembles:

Phase 1 & 2: Linguistic Preprocessing (src/preprocessing.py)
  • Unicode NFC Normalization: Decomposed combining matras are canonicalized into single codepoints to prevent feature fragmentation.
  • Zero-Width & Punctuation Sanitization: Strips mobile zero-width joiners/non-joiners (​, ‌, ‍); normalizes ASCII pipes | to authentic Indic dandas (।); preserves Chandrabindu (ँ, ঁ) and Nuktas.
  • Emoji Preservation: Isolates SMP plane emojis (U+10000–U+10FFFF) with whitespace padding to prevent boundary fusion with adjacent words.
Phase 3: Dual-Level TF-IDF Feature Engineering (src/baselines.py)

Default tokenizers (e.g., Scikit-Learn (?u)\w\w+) delete emojis and sever Indic vowel signs. We built a custom indic_tokenize() plugged into a FeatureUnion combining word n-grams (1–2, 25,000 max features) and subword character n-grams within word boundaries (char_wb, 2–4, 25,000 max features), yielding a leak-free 40,260-dimensional feature space strictly fit on Train data.

Phase 4 & 5: Model Zoo — Classical Baselines & Google MuRIL Transformer
Classical ML Baselines:
  • Complement Naive Bayes (CNB): Inverts class frequencies; robust to subtle skew.
  • Logistic Regression (LR): L2 regularized, balanced class weighting.
  • Linear SVC: Maximum margin hyperplane with hinge loss.
  • Random Forest: 200 estimators for non-linear feature bagging.
Transformer: Google MuRIL (src/transformers_train.py):
  • Pretrained BERT architecture on 17 Indic languages (110M params).
  • Vocabulary Expansion: Added 12 missing emojis to tokenizer; resized embedding layer from 197,285 to 197,297 rows.
  • Fine-tuned on Tesla T4 GPU (AdamW, lr=2e-5, linear warmup, 3 epochs).
Phase 6: Soft-Voting Ensembles & Cross-Lingual Evaluation (src/ensemble.py)

Fuses probability distributions: (1) Classical Ensemble: LR (50%) + CNB (50%); (2) Full Hybrid: MuRIL (70%) + LR (30%). Evaluated cross-lingual transfer across Hindi and Bengali zero-shot splits.

4 Key Challenges Encountered & Engineering Solutions

1. Windows CRT cp1252 Terminal Crashes: Python threw UnicodeEncodeError printing Indic text. Fixed globally via sys.stdout.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8").
2. Pandas Label Corruption on NONE: pd.read_csv() converted stance "NONE" into NaN floats. Solved with keep_default_na=False across all scripts.
3. Pretrained MuRIL Emoji Vocabulary Collapse: Base MuRIL lacked social emojis, collapsing them into [UNK]. We expanded the vocabulary (+12 tokens) and resized the embedding table to 197,297 rows.
4. Scikit-Learn Regex Stripping Matras: Default word regex broke Indic conjuncts. Solved via custom regex with token_pattern=None.
